# v1.1 physical-service selection clarification

Proposed under Ruling17 and Ruling18; controller confirmation precedes integration. This is a wire-contract decision, not restaurant method qualification or a family shipment claim.

`mixture.members` retains ordered objects `{instance_key, weight}` for programme and derived-result owners only. Its existing normalized programme-weight sum of one is unchanged when nonempty and `composition_id` is null. Physical-service keys are rejected in this array; a booster can therefore never enter the area-weight denominator or reduce a programme's energy participation.

New optional `mixture.service_members` is an ordered, unique, nonempty array of strict objects `{instance_key}` referring only to already generated physical-service owners. It selects whole fixtures once, with whole participation one and each fixture's explicit source-basis extent divided by the explicit result denominator. There is no service weight or multiplicity selector. Physical-service multiplicity remains one in the source-qualified whole-owner contract. An unknown `weight`, `enabled`, or disable field is an invalid selection, including an attempted zero service weight; it cannot disable an active programme's required link.

For a service-only generic mixture, `members` is explicitly empty and `service_members` is nonempty. At least one array must select an owner. A physical result requires explicit `result_basis` and positive matching `denominator`; no geometry default is introduced. Existing v1.0 schemas, selections and validation remain unchanged. Exact source-composition member/order/area validation remains binding; explicit service selection cannot modify a locked source-composition recipe's authoritative member allocation.

After request selection validation, positive programme members close their source-qualified required active service links to already generated and validated physical owners. Zero-weight programmes create no closure. Each outgoing link identifies the exact source SID, physical ID, service owner key, operating group, binding index and scoped schedule. Missing or conflicting source/physical/group/extent/budget/trajectory/whole-mode proof fails contextually. Shared aliases and explicit-plus-linked selections deduplicate by exact physical identity and complete fixture proof; duplicate keys in the explicit selector are rejected rather than silently collapsed.

The composer maintains two separate collections: original ordered programme members with unchanged weights, and ordered whole service selections (explicit first, then first encountered positive-programme link order). Whole-service selections enter load aggregation with participation one; they do not enter programme controls or people pairs. Source whole-fixture extent/denominator semantics and physical-ID conflict checks are retained. No new owner, schedule, draw or RNG allocation is created. Unallocated boosters without required programme links remain explicit-only.

The derived generation receipt discloses the original programme members, original explicit service selectors, ordered effective service selections and closure links (`programme_instance_key`, reason, source SID, physical ID, service owner key and explicit/link dedup status). All generated component references retain typed identities and pass the strict local/global binding-reference validation. API and CLI share this exact request contract.

Tests precede integration: explicit service selection leaves programme energy weights unchanged; service-only results; service key in weighted members and service weight/disable fields reject; programme-only required-link selection closes once; explicit-plus-linked and shared-alias dedup; zero programme excludes closure; missing/mispointed/wrong SID/group/extent links fail; unrelated booster remains excluded; annual volume and all nested references close; v1.0 remains unchanged.
