# Residential program input recovery

Date: 2026-10-11. Source: ResStock commit
`dd25369f41a83a0767aefeeac0b6f8a0b0edd649`; OpenStudio
`3.10.0+86d7e215a1`. This is a program-input overlay, not a full building
simulation or a replacement for an immutable atlas release.

`python -m scripts.recover_program_inputs` generates
`data/program-inputs/residential-v1.json`. The Python driver verifies the source
archive, all loaded Ruby/option-table checksums, frozen annual profiles, weather
proxies and OpenStudio executable before executing targeted Ruby calculations.
It retrieves the post-HPXML usage-calibration code from the locked archive:
the earlier schedule-only extraction omitted that file. The raw archive and
existing releases are unchanged. Upstream license notices remain in
`sources/licenses/resstock.txt` and the locked source archive.

## Recovered inputs

All 41 fixtures have records. There are 594 demand components, of which 532
have positive magnitudes, and 12 recovered fixed annual schedule DTOs. Existing
annual channels keep the schedule IDs generated by `residential_definitions.py`;
the supplemental schedule map supplies references for refrigeration, exterior
lighting and miscellaneous source defaults. The source option bindings, their
row locators, original values, expanded option-table properties, source hashes,
transformation and extraction date remain available in metadata/evidence.

- People: executed source activity is 117.228428 W/person, sensible fraction
  3716/6600, native People radiant field 0.558 times that sensible fraction.
- Appliances: washing, drying, dishwasher, cooking, primary/extra refrigeration
  and freezer use the selected option-table characteristics and executed source
  defaults/formulas. Fuel drying/cooking retain both electrical auxiliaries and
  separate natural-gas/propane demands.
- Miscellaneous demands: plugs, television, well pumps, pool/spa pumps and
  heaters, gas grills, gas lighting and fireplaces use source defaults and
  fixed schedules where the stochastic release has no channel.
- Lighting: interior/exterior use the source CFA-bin mapping, not a midpoint;
  garage lighting uses the exact selected garage dimensions. These source
  areas only evaluate the load recipe and do not supply consumer geometry.
- Ceiling fans use the source count/power defaults and the same checksum-locked
  station-proxy weather as the existing realization. Six selected fan fixtures
  have zero annual demand because no month exceeds the source 63 F threshold.
- Water: fixture mixed-water target is 105 F (40.55555555555556 C); washer and
  dishwasher inherit the source 125 F supply target (51.666666666666664 C).
  Delivered draw volumes come from the executed appliance/fixture formulas.

The exact `ResStockArgumentsPostHPXML` usage block is executed, preserving
selected option multipliers plus distinct primary-fridge, extra-fridge,
freezer, appliance, plug and water usage factors. In particular, extra-fridge
calibration is approximately 1.7–2.0 and freezer calibration approximately
1.02–1.39 for these fixtures; they cannot be replaced with 1.

The fixed-background variant extends to extra refrigerators using their own
source-table rows. This preserves the approved no-temperature-feedback
interpretation; it does not claim equivalence to a runtime temperature-driven
refrigerator schedule. Exterior lighting has no indoor thermal gains. Native
garage/basement hosts are retained. Source miscellaneous demands with no zone
gains keep the translator's arbitrary conditioned-space assignment and lost
fraction of 1 rather than creating indoor heat gains.

The three zero-occupant fixtures retain their installed refrigeration. Source
operational appliance/plug/water/lighting skips remain zero and do not require
an artificial positive schedule. Selected-absent appliances are omitted as
active demands. One fixture, `residential_archetype-ecfe7293c2d436ef552d`, selects
hot-tub `Other Fuel`; the exact lookup maps it to `misc_permanent_spa=None`.
`source_option_disabled` records this source-model choice explicitly; it is
not an assertion that the real stock fixture has no spa energy use.

Only electric vehicles are explicitly excluded by the frozen schedule scope.
Other formerly unlisted selected loads have been recovered, not relabeled as
excluded.

## Conversion and verification

Peak W/dwelling = annual kWh * 1000 / schedule equivalent full-load hours.
Peak water m3/s/dwelling = daily US gallons * 365 * 0.003785411784 /
(schedule equivalent full-load hours * 3600). Annual profiles use the existing
2007 hourly local-standard-time calendar. Source-native heat fractions are
retained before the exporter's annual-energy-weighted equipment aggregation.

`python -m unittest tests.test_program_recovery` passes four tests covering
fixture coverage, SI magnitudes, schedule closure and annual conservation,
heat-fraction bounds, source miscellaneous-demand presence, vacancy
refrigeration and fuel auxiliaries. A separate stock-calibration regression
failed before the extra-fridge/freezer multipliers were corrected.

Two independent recovery runs were byte-identical with SHA-256
`14723d8d633cc82a2c93fdd89352ffd099865e52d1c9c84c539c76557c15fb34`
using explicit LF output, matching Git's canonical bytes.
OpenStudio reports existing EPW warnings for an out-of-range wind speed and
missing design-condition fields; fan recovery uses monthly dry-bulb data and
does not use those fields. No EnergyPlus simulation was run.

## Boundary to full-model translation

There are no unresolved magnitudes within the recovered demand inventory.
The source also defines non-metered general-water-use sensible/latent gains
(`InternalGains.apply_general_water_use`), including negative sensible energy,
and water-system distribution waste, recirculation and solar-fraction treatment.
The user explicitly left both additional water effects outside program scope
on 2026-10-11. Those translator/system effects are not silently folded into
electrical demand or delivered fixture draws here. A full translator-equivalence
claim requires separate treatment of those effects, HVAC and envelope systems,
and the existing nominal-control/weather-proxy limitations.
