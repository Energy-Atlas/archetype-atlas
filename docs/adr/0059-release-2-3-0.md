also approved every decision below that is not marked as an agent decision). It publishes what stages AT1

## 1. The delta

Release 2.3.0 is a delta on 2.2.0 (ADR 0036 section 2, ADR 0046): `data/atlas-releases/v2.3.0` holds 1,986 files
and the manifest, and `removed` lists 1,562 paths of 2.2.0. The writer is `python -m scripts.atlas_delta --write
--release 2.3.0`; `--check --release 2.3.0` rebuilds every file from its inputs and requires every program view
to assemble (2,847).

- **The record edits** of ADR 0053: `scripts.atlas_edits.edit(files('2.2.0'))` gives the 1,883 files it changes
  or adds and the 1,562 paths it removes, which are the old names of the records whose IDs move (1,550
  programs, 12 construction sets). The citing files follow the new IDs, among them the 2.2.0 `generation.json`
  blocks, `definition-map.json` and `exporter-inputs.json`.
- **`id-map-2.3.0.json`** at the release root (`atlas_edits.id_map_bytes`): every 2.2.0 ID that moves, mapped
  to its 2.3.0 ID (`ids`, 1,562 entries), with `base_release` 2.2.0 and `release` 2.3.0. *Agent decision:* the
  file is named by its release rather than overlaying `id-map.json`. 2.0.0's `id-map.json` maps the IDs of
  1.6.0 and of 2.0.0 as first committed into 2.0.0, and overlaying it would drop that map from the resolved
  release. A consumer that keeps 2.2.0 IDs (they equal 2.0.0's, ADR 0051 section 4) translates them through
  this file.
- **`generation.json` of the 84 IECC commercial and 15 detailed office folders** (ADR 0055), new in this
  release. The IDs in them are 2.3.0's, so they are derived over the edited release. The writer derives them
  with `scripts.build_zone_recipes.build` over the edited files held in memory, the same function that
  `python -m scripts.build_zone_recipes --out` writes with, and gates them (`gate`). `build_zone_recipes
  --check` over 2.3.0 now requires the shipped documents to equal the derived ones.
- **`LICENSE` and `LICENSE-DATA`** (section 2) and the delta's `README.md`.
- **The writer** is the tagged `# >>> release 2.3.0` block of `scripts/atlas_delta.py`, the mechanism of ADR
  0040 decision 17 that ADR 0056 started: release 2.2.0 pins the file without the block (`pinned_writer`), and
  2.3.0 pins it with the block. `input_sha256` also pins `scripts/atlas_edits.py`, its two inputs
  (`data/program-inputs/pnnl-point-load-exceptions-v1.json`, `distinguishing-names-v1.json`), both licence files
  and the 2.2.0 manifest. `counts.moved_ids` gives the moved programs and sets and names the ID map.
  `counts.generation` covers the resolved release: 189 folders, 2,270 programs with blocks (1,765
  `comstock_hours`, 422 `composition`, 22 `fixed`, 20 `resstock_unit`, 41 `resstock_dwelling`), 2,847 program
  views, 507 services, 219 configurations. `deferred` states the generation coverage by folder class (ADR 0055):
  the 66 folders without blocks are 36 commercial folders without a sampling type, 12 IECC apartment folders
  and 18 residential prototypes.
- `release_editor --check` now also re-derives every 2.3.0 program ID (read with its generation block) and
  every set ID, and none moves.

## 2. The licence split (issue #44)

The owner decided on 2026-10-30 to split the licence files at this release rather than edit published
manifests.

- The root `LICENSE` is the MIT text alone, so GitHub detects `MIT`. `LICENSE-DATA` is the Creative Commons
  Attribution 4.0 International legal code. *Agent decision:* the text is the SPDX license-list copy of the
  legal code, as REUSE packages it, with its line endings normalised to LF. ADR 0048's scope text (what is code,
  what is data and documentation, and what third-party material is excluded) moves into the README's
  *Licence* section. The grants are unchanged.
- Releases 2.1.0 and 2.2.0 each packaged the two-part `LICENSE` and pinned it as their licence input. Their
  writers now take the copy each release packaged as that input, so the check stands on the release's own
  checksum, the verify-only rule of ADR 0046 section 7 for an input that left the tree. `--check` of 2.1.0
  and 2.2.0 still reproduces both folders byte for byte.
- Release 2.3.0 packages both files. The site writes `notices/original-work.txt` (MIT) and
  `notices/original-data.txt` (CC BY 4.0). The `atlas-schedules` wheel bundles release data, so its licence
  expression is `MIT AND CC-BY-4.0`, with `license-files` `LICENSE` and `LICENSE-DATA`. `CITATION.cff` names
  2.3.0 and still lists both licences.

## 4. The origin of the daylighting fractions (review F5, part c)

**Context.** The 90.1-2019 medium office whole-building program is climate-independent (`climate` null), yet
its evidence row cites the climate 0A (Ho Chi Minh) prototype for its daylighting fractions (review S3-15).
`scripts.lighting_controls` takes, for each program, the most common daylighting record across the 19 PNNL
prototype locations and cites the first location in climate order that gives it. For this program all 19
agree, so 0A is one copy among 19, not a choice.

**Decision.** Program JSON 6.0.0 gains `lighting_controls.daylighting_origin`, given by the exporter on every
known block and derived from the record and its evidence. The program record does not change, so no ID moves.
- `kind`: `prototype_zone_set` means the fractions are geometry-derived from the `Daylighting:Controls` of the
  PNNL prototype zones the program was extracted from. `reference_model_scan` means no DOE reference model has
  controls, a source zero for any geometry.
- `climates`: the prototype climates whose model gives `daylighting`. The others are in
  `daylighting_by_climate`.
- `cited_climate`: the climate of the model the evidence row cites.
- `note`: the same in words. Where all 19 climates agree, it says the cited model is a copy, not a choice.

The medium office whole building reads `prototype_zone_set`, 19 climates, cited 0A, a copy of all 19. The
field is optional, a minor-class change (`docs/schema-versioning.md`). 6.0.0 was not yet published, so the
field is made in 6.0.0 before its first release and recorded as revision 2 of the schema in
`schemas/revisions.json`. The lighting-controls guide shows it.

## 6. LOD study drop 3 (amends ADR 0050 sections 4 and 6)

The drop is BEMGen stage LOD1 (D-219 to D-224, BEMGen `8d2e3bd`, the merge of its pull request 22).
`research/lod-study/results/lod-recommendations.json` (R3, schema 1.2.0, SHA-256 `439fc0a4…`),
`lod-recommendations-ht.json` (R4 with the small-hotel rerun, `ca89a7d7…`) and `provenance.json` (`88f5cb29…`)
are copied byte for byte into `data/lod-recommendations/v1/`, with no normalisation, since BEMGen now writes
LF (D-223). BEMGen's `provenance.json` is copied as `bemgen-provenance.json` beside the atlas sidecar
`provenance.json` (drop 3, which names its digest). `scripts/lod-study/schemas/lod-recommendations-v1.schema.json`
1.2.0 (`adbce622…`) replaces the copy in `schemas/` byte for byte. It is a minor bump, so `MAJOR` stays `1` and
the loader reads 1.0.0 to 1.2.0.

The pages, generated from the files:
- **Refused zonings are not generated, not failed.** Schema 1.2.0 lists every planned zoning. The 1,056 R3
  zonings and 352 R4 zonings that the generator refuses by a documented limit (`status` Invalid: multiplied
  rooms, a plan too narrow for the perimeter depth) are listed under each cell as *not generated* and never
  as failed runs. Counts of failed runs, test sites and zonings per cell count generated zonings only.
- **Finer than the reference.** A recommendation has at most the reference's zone count (D-220), so the four
  full-service restaurant 4A cells have none. The pages flag them: the summary row, the cell's sentence and the
  prototype's facts, and the method page lists them. A zoning that meets the threshold with more zones than
  the reference is marked *yes, finer than the reference*. R3 has 214 of 264 cells with a recommendation.
- **The robust rule takes the same bound** (amends ADR 0050 section 6, agent decision for consistency with
  D-220). A candidate must have at most the reference's zone count at each orientation, so the restaurant 4A
  pairs have no robust zoning: 107 of 132 pairs are robust (109 in drop 2).
- **The sub-study has no failed run** (D-224, after BEMGen's heat-transfer rule D-211). D-201's "not
  applicable" caution and its small-hotel text go. The heat-transfer page says every generated zoning ran.
- **Deduplication keeps the both-on twin** (D-221). The cautions say that an off setting left in a
  recommendation is a tie with a zoning that has both on, not a twin (8 of 18 sub-study cells).
- **Cost medians** (D-222). A prototype's figure is the median over its distinct timed test sites (137 twins
  dropped). A zoning's figure and the overall figure are medians of the prototype medians, each timed
  prototype one vote. The overview shows the overall figures (processor-time ratio 0.659 over 9 prototypes)
  and the number of prototypes per zoning.
- The method page states the unjudged loads and the threshold sensitivity the file carries (D-223).
- **Links by the site release's IDs** (*agent decision*). The study's program IDs are atlas 2.0.0's, and 1,550
  programs have moved by 2.3.0. The pages keep the files as written. They link each program by its 2.3.0 ID,
  translated through the `id-map-<release>.json` files after the study's inputs release
  (`lod_site.id_translation`), and say so.

## 7. The site

The site builds release 2.3.0. The 99 folders that ADR 0055 generates now carry their settings, so the reason
"release 2.2.0 predates their blocks" leaves the site index. `suite_site.generation_reason` now treats a folder
of a generated class without blocks as an error.

## 8. Consequences

- Consumers move to release 2.3.0 and its contracts (section 3) and re-key stored 2.2.0 IDs through
  `id-map-2.3.0.json` (BEMGen stage REL-B).
- CI checks all three deltas: `atlas_delta --check` for 2.1.0, 2.2.0 and 2.3.0, and `build_zone_recipes
  --check`. The data-release workflow verifies 2.2.0 as well.
- Later releases follow the same pattern: a tagged block in `scripts/atlas_delta.py` after this one, and an ID
  map named by its release when IDs move.
